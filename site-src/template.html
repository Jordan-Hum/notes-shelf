    Offerings

    Call threshold: how far the underlying must be above its starting level on a call date for the note to be called early (≥ 0% = at or above the start).
    Coupon threshold: the lowest the underlying can fall, versus its start, and still pay that period's contingent coupon.
    Barrier: the level checked at maturity. At or above it, principal is returned; below it, the loss follows the underlying.

    Data is a snapshot read from notes.cibc.com (principal-at-risk and principal-protected current offerings), National Bank's Class F current-offer page and its product pages, bmonotes.com (principal-at-risk and principal-protected notes currently selling), and Scotiabank GBM's current issues (principal-at-risk, principal-protected and market-linked GICs). Thresholds are shown relative to each underlying's starting level; where an issuer states a level as a % of initial (e.g. 105%), it is shown here as the move from the start (+5%). National Bank omits the call threshold on its offer page when it is 0%; its product pages confirm this. "Return p.a." means the annual contingent coupon for income notes, the per-year call premium for autocallables, and the annualized booster or fixed coupon where a bank publishes one; growth notes without a fixed figure show their participation in the details. Fee is the selling concession per $100 where published (Class F carries none). Always confirm terms against the issuer's pricing supplement before recommending a note.
